## Semantic Teaching Insights

Students mainly perceived learning around hashing/collisions and heaps, with dictionaries and related implementation ideas as secondary gains. The most actionable struggle pattern is not simple terminology confusion but choosing and applying structures in realistic contexts, especially for heap variants and hashed structures. Heaps are the clearest mixed-confidence topic because they appear in both learned and struggled responses. Next instruction should emphasize worked use cases, side-by-side structure selection, and concrete mechanics for hashing/collision handling.

### Top learned concepts

- Hashing and hash tables, especially search efficiency and collision handling, were the clearest perceived learning gains.
- Heaps were a major takeaway, including binary heaps and binomial heaps.
- Students also reported learning related dictionary/associative-array ideas and their operations.
- A smaller cluster connected this week’s material to implementation topics such as caching, probing, and chaining.

### Top struggle clusters

- Heaps remain the most visible content-area struggle, suggesting students learned the term/topic but may not yet distinguish variants or know when to use them.
- Several students were not stuck on definitions so much as on applications: when to use particular data structures, what heap variants are for, and what practical scenarios motivate hashing/caching choices.
- Some reflections indicate uncertainty around hashed indexing and associative arrays, pointing to lingering confusion about core mechanics.
- A nontrivial cluster reported no specific questions or indicated limited engagement/time, so absence of struggle should not be interpreted as mastery.

### Future interest clusters

- Students want more depth on heaps.
- There is continued interest in hashing and related data-structure topics.
- Many future-interest responses ask for application-oriented follow-through: realistic algorithms, larger-project context, coding-interview style use, and practical use cases.
- There is some interest in broader data-structures context, including older/less-used structures and more practice with dictionaries.

### Mismatches

- Hashing appears strongly in perceived learning but not strongly in struggled terms, which may mean students feel they grasped the basics while still needing application-focused reinforcement.
- Heaps appear in both learned and struggled responses, marking them as a high-salience topic with mixed confidence.
- Difficulty is moderate overall (mean 5.62, median 6), but reflection text includes several 'no questions' or low-engagement responses, so the difficulty number likely blends true understanding, uncertainty, and uneven participation.
- Future-interest signals lean toward practical application and project context more than toward deeper theory alone, which is not fully mirrored by the top learned terms.

### Teaching adjustments

- Use a compare-and-choose activity for heaps and related structures: given a workload or scenario, have students decide which structure fits and justify the tradeoffs.
- Add one short application block that ties hashing, dictionaries, caching, and collision handling to a realistic system or project pipeline.
- Reinforce associative arrays/hashed indexing with a concrete walkthrough showing key-to-bucket mapping, collisions, probing, and chaining in one end-to-end example.
- Because heaps were both learned and struggled with, include a brief recap that distinguishes binary heaps, binomial heaps, and their use cases without assuming prior clarity.
- Provide one or two 'why this matters' examples for each structure covered, since practical applications were a recurring request.
- Consider low-stakes practice prompts or attendance-independent check-ins, since some responses suggest students may be relying on peers/readings rather than lecture engagement.

### Suggested follow-up exit-ticket questions

- Given a specific task (priority queue, fast lookup, caching, ordered retrieval), which data structure would you choose and why?
- What part of heaps is still least clear: structure, operations, implementation, or use cases?
- Can you explain the difference between collision resolution by chaining and probing in your own words?
- What is one realistic application of hashing, dictionaries, or caching that now makes more sense to you?
- Which data structure from this week do you feel most confident applying, and which do you want one more example of?
